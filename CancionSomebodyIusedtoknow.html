📐 Grammar Focus: The "Used to" Family

📖 Cambridge Dictionary ↗

Students often confuse these three structures. Let's break them down clearly:

1. Used to + Infinitive

Use: For past habits or states that are no longer true now.

"We used to talk every single day."

2. Be used to + -ing / Noun

Use: To be accustomed to something; it's normal for you.

"Now she is used to living without him."

3. Get used to + -ing / Noun

Use: The process of becoming accustomed to something new.

"It takes time to get used to being single again."

💡 Key Expressions & British Pronunciation (UK)

📖 Dictionary Helper ↗

Listen to the native British audio, study the definitions, and review practical examples.

1. "Somebody that I used to know"
📖 Dictionary

Meaning: A person you had an intimate relationship with in the past, but now they are a total stranger.

Example: "We shared an apartment for years, but now we don't speak; she's just somebody that I used to know."

2. "Cut somebody out"
📖 Dictionary

Meaning: To stop communicating with someone completely or remove them from your life.

Example: "After constant arguments, he decided to cut her out of his social media and phone contacts."

3. "Treat your data like you had no change"
📖 Dictionary

Meaning: Acting coldly, pretending nothing happened, or treating an ex-partner as if they never mattered.

Example: "I said hello on the street, but she looked right through me and treated me like a total stranger."
