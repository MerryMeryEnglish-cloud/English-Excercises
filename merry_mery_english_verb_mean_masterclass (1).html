The Multiple Personalities of "MEAN"

Listen to standard pronunciation and explore how the verb shifts meanings depending on context.

Look up in Cambridge Dictionary
Click audio icons to listen
1

Intent / Plan

mean + to-infinitive

Used to express intention, purpose, or aim.

"Sorry, I didn't mean to disturb you while you were studying."
2

Definition

mean + noun / clause

Conveys definition, sense, or translation of a word or sign.

"What does 'fortnight' mean? It means fourteen days."
3

Significance

mean + a lot / something to

Expresses emotional value or importance to someone.

"Your thoughtful advice means a lot to me."
4

Consequence

mean + gerund (-ing)

Indicates what an action involves, requires, or causes.

"Accepting this role will mean spending more time queueing in traffic."

Grammar Focus: Infinitive vs. Gerund

Notice the shift in meaning between these two verb patterns!

MEAN + TO + VERB Intention

Refers to a person's deliberate intention or conscious plan.

• "I meant to call you yesterday, but I lost track of time."

(It was my intention, but I failed to do it.)

MEAN + VERB-ING Involvement / Result

Refers to what a situation or action naturally entails or causes.

• "Catching the 7 AM train means getting up at five."

(The action inevitably involves waking up early.)
